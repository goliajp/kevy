kevy 7.0.0 · Queues & jobs

Hand work to a worker,
and get it back if the worker dies

A queue table in a relational database is a locking convention with extra steps. kevy has two real queues: a list when losing a job is survivable, and a stream with consumer groups when it is not.

Which of the two

Use a list when the job is cheap to redo and the worker is unlikely to die mid-task — sending an email, warming a cache, invalidating a CDN path. BRPOP blocks until there is work, so the worker does not poll.

Use a stream when the job must not be lost. A consumer group hands each message to exactly one worker and remembers that it did. If the worker dies before acknowledging, the message stays in the pending list and another worker can claim it — that is the whole reason streams exist, and it is the difference between a queue and a hope.

A list, for work you can redo

Producers push; a blocked worker wakes the moment there is work. Two commands, no polling loop, no scheduler.

Producer: push the job
LPUSH jobs:email '{"to":"ada@example.com","tpl":"welcome"}'
-> (integer) 1
Worker: block until there is one
BRPOP jobs:email 30
-> 1) "jobs:email"
   2) "{\"to\":\"ada@example.com\",\"tpl\":\"welcome\"}"

No polling loop, no sleep, no thundering herd — the pop returns the instant a job arrives, or after 30 seconds empty-handed.

Delayed jobs: the score is the due time
ZADD jobs:due 1783875499 '{"id":"j-91"}'
-> (integer) 1
ZPOPMIN.BELOW jobs:due 1783875500
-> the job payload, only if it is due

ZPOPMIN.BELOW is kevy's own — it takes only what is actually due and stops at the first job that is not.

Cost & limits A popped job that its worker never finishes is gone. That is the trade for this being two commands — take it only for work you can redo. And on a multi-shard server, BLPOP across several keys does not honour Redis's strict left-to-right priority: keys on the connection's own shard are served first.

A stream, for work you cannot lose

Each job goes to exactly one worker and stays pending until acknowledged. A dead worker's job is reclaimable, with its full history intact.

Once, at setup: create the group
XGROUP CREATE jobs:pay g1 $ MKSTREAM
-> OK
Producer: append the job
XADD jobs:pay * order 4410 amount 8400
-> "1783875499458-0"
Worker: read, work, then acknowledge
XREADGROUP GROUP g1 worker-3 COUNT 1 BLOCK 5000 STREAMS jobs:pay >
XACK jobs:pay g1 1783875499458-0

The ID you acknowledge is the one XREADGROUP handed you. Until the XACK, the job is pending — on you, on the record.

A worker died before XACK: reclaim its job
XAUTOCLAIM jobs:pay g1 worker-7 60000 0-0
# claims anything idle for more than 60 s

XPENDING jobs:pay g1
# what is still outstanding, and who has it

Cost & limits A stream is not free. Trimming with MAXLEN recomputes the stream's weight, which is O(N) in the whole stream — trim on a schedule, not on every XADD. And XREADGROUP's COUNT bounds what you are handed, not what is scanned: the entire undelivered tail is materialised first. Per-command detail is in the reference.
